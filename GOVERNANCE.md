# Community Governance

This repository is the public community surface for UND Industries. Its purpose is to collect useful feedback, support requests, bug reports, feature ideas, and community contributions without exposing UND's private development systems.

## Decision model

Community input is encouraged and may inform product decisions, but submitting an idea or receiving community support does not guarantee implementation, timing, or inclusion in a roadmap.

UND maintainers are responsible for final decisions about repository policy, moderation, issue status, accepted changes, and product direction.

## Triage

Incoming items should be handled by type:

- **Bug reports:** confirm scope, reproduction steps, affected product/version, and whether the report contains sensitive information.
- **Feature requests:** identify the user problem, affected product, expected benefit, and whether the request is specific enough to become actionable work.
- **Product feedback:** capture the underlying user experience and link to an actionable issue if a concrete change is identified.
- **Support requests:** answer publicly only when the required information is safe to share publicly. Move sensitive account or security matters to a private channel.
- **Security reports:** never investigate sensitive exploit details in a public thread; follow `SECURITY.md`.

## Status and closure

Issues may be closed when they are resolved, duplicated, no longer applicable, lack enough information after reasonable follow-up, belong in another channel, or create security/privacy risk by remaining public.

Closing an issue does not necessarily mean the underlying idea was rejected; it may mean the public issue is no longer the right tracking surface.

## Moderation

Content may be edited, hidden, locked, or removed when necessary to protect users, prevent abuse or spam, remove sensitive data, keep conversations on topic, or enforce `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`.

## Public/private boundary

This repository must not become a mirror of UND's private engineering repositories. Public community artifacts should contain only the minimum technical detail needed for users to report problems, share ideas, and understand public-facing processes.
